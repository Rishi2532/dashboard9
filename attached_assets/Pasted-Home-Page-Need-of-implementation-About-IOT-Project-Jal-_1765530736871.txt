Need of implementation / About IOT Project  

 

Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) is a flagship initiative launched by the Government of India, aimed at ensuring “Har Ghar Jal” through Functional Household Tap Connections (FHTCs). The mission envisions that every rural household should have access to adequate quantity (55 litres per capita per day - LPCD) of prescribed quality drinking water on a regular basis, at affordable service delivery charges. This initiative seeks to improve the living standards of rural communities by ensuring safe and reliable drinking water supply. 

In Maharashtra, State Water and Sanitation Mission, Water Supply and Sanitation Department, Government of Maharashtra; is implementing JJM across the state. Maharashtra Jeevan Pradhikaran (MJP) and Zilla Parishad (ZP) are playing key roles as implementation agencies for the Jal Jeevan Mission. Considering the need for real-time monitoring, effective management, and transparency in service delivery, the SWSM has initiated the implementation of Internet of Things (IoT) solutions across the state under JJM. 

By implementation of IoT solutions, State Water and Sanitation Mission (SWSM), Maharashtra has significantly enhanced the ability to monitor, manage, and optimize rural water supply schemes under the Jal Jeevan Mission. By integrating smart infrastructure and centralized control, SWSM is ensuring that every home gets clean and reliable water, supporting the goal of 'Har Ghar Jal' in a safe and sustainable way. Primary goal of integrating IoT into the water distribution system is to: 

Monitor water supply schemes effectively 

Ensure adequate quantity and quality 

Streamline operations 

Detect issues such as water shortages or contamination 

Enhance the efficiency and accountability of the rural water supply system

SWSM’s IoT implementation in Maharashtra under the JJM has been divided into two main parts: 

 

Part I 

Designing, Engineering, Procurement, Supply, Packaging, Transportation & Delivery 

Installation, Testing & Commissioning 

Integration with the Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) established at the State JJM Office, Belapur 

Maintenance of IoT solutions at the village level 

 

 

Part II 

Design, implementation, and maintenance of a centralized IoT platform at the state level for monitoring the actual field-level data shared by installed IoT devices at the field level.

Key Features of the IoT devices: 

Flow Meter (FM): 

Flow meters used in JJM schemes are designed for high-precision measurement of water flow, performing reliably across both high and low flow conditions. These flow meters are integrated with the Integrated Command & Control Centre (ICCC), enabling remote access to real-time data for monitoring, troubleshooting, and performance analysis through platforms like AVEVA PI. It uses Modbus and MQTT protocols for reliable communication, even in remote areas with limited infrastructure. System supports regular data logging, facilitating trend analysis and enabling calculation of per capita water supply based on total volume delivered and village population.

Residual Chlorine Analyser (RCA): 

Residual Chlorine Analyser (RCA) plays a vital role in monitoring water quality by measuring the concentration of free residual chlorine in the distribution system, typically within the range of 0.2 mg/L to 0.5 mg/L. It uses advanced amperometry sensors to provide accurate and reliable readings. RCAs are equipped with automated data logging features that transmit real-time data to the Integrated Command & Control Centre (ICCC) having AVEVA PI as an application layer, allowing continuous monitoring to ensure chlorination levels remain within safe limits. AVEVA PI system support instant detection of deviations and enable timely corrective action by triggering the incidence to concern authority, thereby enhancing the overall safety and quality of drinking water.

Pressure Sensor: 

Pressure sensors installed at the farthest points or highest elevations of the water distribution network, typically operating in the range of 0.2 to 0.7 Bar, are essential for assessing pressure adequacy at critical locations. These sensors are built to withstand environmental variations such as temperature changes and humidity. The pressure data is integrated into the centralized AVEVA PI System at the Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC), enabling real-time monitoring. This data supports automated daily, weekly, and monthly pressure reports, which are used for ongoing analysis, alerts, and operational decision-making.

Data is routed through MQTT adapters acting as the interface between field devices and the backend system 

A load balancer manages the data stream from all villages 

These sensors collect real-time data and transmit it to the centralized cloud server, enabling data analytics and visualization. 

 

Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) by SWSM:  

To support centralized monitoring, SWSM has established an Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) at the state level in Belapur. ICCC is equipped with the required hardware and visualization, monitoring software application tools.  

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

Application Features are as follows: 

State-wise, region-wise, scheme-wise, and ESR-wise monitoring 

Visualization of key parameters like flow, pressure, and water quality 

Real-time data filtering and dashboards for operational insight 

Anomaly detection and automatic alerts through: 

SMS 

In-app notifications 

Emails 

 

Escalation Matrix: On detecting issues, alerts are automatically sent to: 

Village-level operators 

Concerned Deputy Engineer and Executive Engineer 

Higher authorities if issues remain unresolved within a defined timeframe
